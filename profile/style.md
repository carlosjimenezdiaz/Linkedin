# Carlos's LinkedIn Voice & Style Guide

> This file is the source of truth for how Carlos writes on LinkedIn.
> Update it as his style evolves. The `/linkedin style` command will propose updates
> automatically after 3+ posts are added to `content/published/`.

---

## Voice

**Direct and technically precise.** Carlos writes like someone who has actually built and deployed these systems — not like someone who read about them. He uses specific terminology correctly and doesn't dumb things down.

**Authoritative without being arrogant.** He states opinions confidently but grounds them in real experience. No hedging. No "in my humble opinion."

**Practical over theoretical.** Even when discussing research, he ties it back to what it means in practice — in a trading system, a risk model, a production ML pipeline.

---

## Tone

- Confident, not boastful
- Curious, not academic
- Candid, not edgy
- Never motivational-poster. Never bro-marketing.

---

## Format Rules

**Hooks (first line):**
- Must work as a standalone sentence — if someone only reads the first line, it still means something
- Best patterns:
  - Counterintuitive claim: `"Most ML models in finance fail before they go live."`
  - Specific number: `"I've built 4 agentic systems in production. Here's what breaks every time."`
  - Sharp question: `"What if your risk model is more dangerous than the risk it measures?"`
- Never start with: "I'm excited to share...", "Thrilled to announce...", "Just published..."

**Body structure — choose one:**
- **The problem → insight → implication** (best for thought leadership)
- **The story arc** (situation → turning point → lesson learned)
- **The list with substance** (numbered, but each point is a real insight, not a platitude)

**Paragraphs:**
- 1–3 sentences max per paragraph
- Leave white space — LinkedIn is a mobile-first platform
- Occasional **bold** for one key phrase per post. Don't overuse.

**Length:** 150–300 words. Never under 100, rarely over 350.

**Hashtags:** 3–5, placed at the very end on their own line. No hashtags mid-post.

---

## CTA Style

Every post should close with a low-friction, relevant CTA. Match the CTA to the post type:

| Post type | CTA style |
|---|---|
| Thought leadership | "Disagree? Drop a comment. I want the pushback." |
| Personal story | "What's your experience with [topic]? Let me know below." |
| News commentary | "Happy to dig into this further — DM me if you're thinking about [topic] for your firm." |
| CTA-focused | "If you're building in [space] and want an outside perspective, let's connect." |

Never say: "Like and share if you found this valuable." Never ask for engagement directly.

---

## Forbidden Patterns

- "I'm humbled/excited/proud to share"
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "The future of X is here"
- "Game-changing" / "revolutionary" / "disruptive"
- Unsubstantiated stats without noting the source
- Excessive emojis (max 2 per post, used sparingly)
- Wall-of-text paragraphs
- Posts that are just a list of buzzwords
