# Template: News Commentary Post

**Use when:** Reacting to a paper, industry announcement, regulatory development, or trending topic. Shows you're current and have a practitioner's perspective that others lack.

**Target length:** 150–250 words

**Goal:** Give your audience a sharper, more informed take than what they'd get from a headline. Be the person who explains *why it matters*.

---

## Structure

```
[HOOK — what happened + why it matters in one sentence]
  Don't just say "X was published." Say what it means.

[THE CONTEXT — 1–2 sentences: brief background for readers who missed it]

[YOUR TAKE — 3–5 sentences: the practitioner's angle]
  - What does this mean for people actually building in this space?
  - What's being underreported or misunderstood?
  - What are the real implications vs. the hype?

[THE CAVEAT or NUANCE — 1–2 sentences: steelman the other view, or note what's still uncertain]

[CTA — invites reaction or signals expertise]

[HASHTAGS]
```

---

## Hook Examples

- "[Paper/announcement] dropped yesterday. The headline is [X]. The part worth paying attention to is [Y]."
- "Regulators just [did X]. Here's what it means for firms using [relevant tech]."
- "Everyone's talking about [thing]. The part nobody's mentioning: [insight]."
- "[Company] just [announced X]. If you're building [related thing], this changes the calculus."
- "New paper from [lab/org]: [title]. Three things that actually matter for practitioners:"

---

## Research Process (use WebSearch before writing)

1. Search for the source material — get the actual announcement or paper abstract
2. Search for practitioner reactions — what are senior people in the field saying?
3. Look for regulatory or compliance angles if relevant
4. Find one data point or specific detail that most commentary is missing

Use the WebSearch tool to ground commentary in accurate, current information.

---

## CTA Options

- "Happy to dig into the implications for [specific use case] — DM me if you're working through this."
- "What's your read on this? Curious how people on the buy-side are thinking about it."
- "If your firm is figuring out how to respond to this, let's talk."
- "Drop a comment if you think I'm missing something — I might be."

## Example Post (illustrative)

```
A new paper on LLM-based financial document analysis just dropped. The headline: "GPT-4 matches junior analyst accuracy."

The part worth reading carefully: the evaluation set was earnings call transcripts with clear numerical anchors. That's the easy case.

The hard cases — covenant analysis in credit agreements, risk factor interpretation in 10-Ks, cross-document reconciliation — aren't in the benchmark.

My take from deploying these systems: LLMs are genuinely useful for structured extraction and summarization. They're unreliable for reasoning across ambiguous, high-stakes clauses where the interpretation matters more than the text.

The useful framing isn't "does it match analysts?" It's "what exactly is it good at, and where do you still need the human?"

If you're evaluating LLM vendors for financial document workflows, happy to share what I've seen actually hold up in production.

#FinancialML #LLMEngineering #AssetManagement #GenerativeAI #AIInFinance
```
